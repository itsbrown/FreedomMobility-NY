A practical guide for homeowners in Western and Central New York.

## 1. Straight vs curved stairlifts

Most homes in newer suburbs around Rochester or Syracuse have straight stairs and can use simpler, lower-cost straight models. Historic homes in Buffalo’s Elmwood Village or Rochester’s Park Avenue often require custom curved rails that follow the staircase shape.

If you are not sure, a photo of the full staircase from top and bottom is enough for us to tell you which category you are in before we visit.

## 2. Indoor vs outdoor models

Outdoor stairlifts are built to withstand snow, ice, and rain — essential for Upstate NY winters. They include weatherproof covers and heated options. Indoor units should not be installed on exposed porches.

## 3. Features that actually matter

- Swivel seat for a safe transfer at the top
- Folding rails and seats for narrow hallways
- Battery backup (critical during power outages common in winter storms)
- Weight capacity (standard 300 lbs, heavy-duty up to 400+ lbs)
- Simple remote or wireless call/send from both landings

Skip gadget checklists. The right lift is the one the person will use several times a day without help.
